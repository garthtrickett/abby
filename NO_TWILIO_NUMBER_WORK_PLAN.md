**Purpose:** close every useful engineering gap from the [core and Cliniko gap analysis](AEVA_ABBY_CORE_CLINIKO_GAP_ANALYSIS.md) that does not require an owned Twilio number. ## What “can do now” means

There are three different kinds of completion:

| Mark | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Close now** | Code, deterministic E2E, local PostgreSQL/browser tests, and any available demo-provider evidence can establish the stated engineering contract without a Twilio number. | A scoped Cliniko availability reader returns provisional times and fails truthfully. |
| **Build now; hold disabled** | Implementation and synthetic/demo tests can be completed, but a separate clinic/product decision or unproven Cliniko behavior prevents activation. | Patient-specific follow-up cannot turn on merely because a demo recall POST succeeds. |
| **Carrier proof later** | The actual behavior depends on a real incoming call, its carrier media, or number-level routing. Synthetic traffic can rehearse it but cannot close it. | Hearing the greeting, interruption-to-silence, and fallback ringing. |

“Clinic sign-off” means an authorized operator for **that clinic** accepts a specific, versioned patient-facing policy and demonstrates that its staff can carry out the associated process. Record the clinic and location scope, approver and role, policy version, decision date, staff owner and backup where relevant, and the result of a rehearsal. This is an operational activation decision, not approval of the code or a special legal certificate. It is **not** needed to write or test generic behavior with synthetic data. The product owner can decide Abby's generic defaults now; a demo account cannot approve another clinic's process.

For example, enabling patient-specific follow-up requires the clinic to choose who monitors the Cliniko queue, how often they check it, who acknowledges and resolves requests, and what happens when the queue or API fails. A successful demo recall write proves none of those steps. Until the clinic accepts and rehearses them, Abby must leave that handoff unavailable and use its truthful caller fallback.

| Decision or evidence | Owner | Can advance without a Twilio number? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Generic caller wording, capability modes and safety rule | Abby product owner | Yes; record in the decision log and cases. |
| A real clinic's verification questions, greeting, staff queue, hours and booking policy | That clinic's authorized operator | Yes, if a participating clinic is available; otherwise build/test with a synthetic policy and leave activation off. |
| Cliniko API behavior, collision/unknown-result handling | Engineering, using bounded demo probes and contract tests | Yes for the tested account; a safe `execute` mode still requires convincing operation-specific evidence. |
| Carrier media, number-level fallback and audible quality | Voice engineering with an owned number and test caller | No; keep this as a separate later gate. |

The work assumes the existing isolated Cliniko demo account and optional owner-only model credentials. Patient-free fixtures and synthetic phone numbers are sufficient for most tasks. Do not put credentials, call audio, transcripts, appointment details, patient-linked outcomes, or staff-work details into this repository or Abby's standalone database. D-06/D-11/D-12 permit durable patient-specific data only as clinic-scoped phone↔Cliniko-ID links. Standalone call state remains transient; an embedded buyer may supply its own authorized durable ledger.

## Starting point: do not rebuild these

- Abby already has a typed SAM core, reference booking/staff/agent command flows, a transient standalone host that disables patient actions, Kanel/Kysely PostgreSQL types, lit-html reference UI, and synthetic E2E tests. The reference host is evidence for composition, not a live Cliniko capability. [Source overview](src/README.md), [disabled host](src/standalone/disabled-host.ts).
- Signed synthetic Twilio HTTP/WSS, local process tests, and a disposable public tunnel through the **actual voice runner** exist. They prove route and media contracts under synthetic traffic, not carrier audio or a configured number. [Voice runbook](phase-1/VOICE_TEST_RUNBOOK.md).
- Cliniko demo probes cover metadata, patient candidates, provisional availability, individual appointment read, synthetic create/cancel/move, and recall experiments. The [evidence register](phase-0/CLINIKO_EVIDENCE.md) specifies what each proved; no probe by itself authorizes automation.
- The last declared synthetic [speech gate](eval/SPEECH_QUALITY_GATE.md) failed: 12/18 noisy and 11/24 safety runs missed critical terms, and 1/40 synthesis jobs was uncertain. Natural-language availability remains disabled. [Baseline](eval/LIVE_BASELINE_2026-09-29.md).

## Work packages in dependency order

## Sequencing and parallel execution

1. **Start A1/A2 and A3 immediately.** They define the corpus and address the current speech blocker. A4/A5 can extend the existing runner in parallel.
2. **Run B1/B2/B4 alongside voice work.** B3 and B6 can be designed and tested with synthetic policy while clinic-specific sign-off is sought. B5 depends on B3's proof contract, not a Twilio number.
3. **Investigate C1 and C2 early.** They decide whether patient requests and booking can ever be offered safely in standalone Cliniko. Do not make C3–C5 user-visible merely because the adapter compiles or the demo API returns success.
4. **Build D1–D4 alongside the integration tracks**, but ship each settings or agent capability with its own full-path E2E. Treat D5 as individually scoped follow-on work.

For every package, record the exact code revision, scenario IDs, test command, result, source/fixture version, capability mode and remaining external decision. Independent suites run in parallel; only operations sharing a real Cliniko demo account, provider quota or physical resource are serialized, with the reason documented. Do not rerun an unchanged failing command or reduce concurrency to hide a race.

## What the Twilio number still gates

These results cannot be claimed from this plan, even if every package above passes:

- Number-level primary and independent fallback configuration, real incoming-call routing, actual fallback ringing and behavior when Abby is down before intake or dies mid-call.
- Audible response latency and quality over the intended carrier, real interruption-to-silence, DTMF/media behavior, transfer connection and hangup behavior.
- The declared representative, consented, patient-free carrier corpus and human listening gate. The [speech gate](eval/SPEECH_QUALITY_GATE.md) requires at least 100 complete calls from at least 10 speakers before patient traffic; synthetic speech results cannot substitute.
- Any claim that a clinic is live-ready. Separate clinic-specific sign-off, verified staff ownership, privacy decisions and safe Cliniko capability modes remain required even after a real call succeeds.

**First no-number milestone:** A1/A2, a passing or explicitly failed A3 with a root-cause record, A4/A5 synthetic public runner, and B1/B2/B4 read contracts. This gives a truthful, reproducible foundation while C1/C2 determine whether booking or request-only work can be enabled at all.
